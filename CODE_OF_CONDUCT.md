# Code of conduct

Vexcalibur project spaces are for useful, respectful work. Disagreement is welcome. Harassment is not.

## Expected conduct

- Address the work and its effect, not the person's worth or motives.
- Give enough context for others to understand a question or decision.
- Accept correction and make room for people with different experience.
- Respect privacy, attribution, and a request to end an off-topic exchange.
- Follow a maintainer's moderation direction when it protects the discussion or another participant.

## Unacceptable conduct

The project does not accept:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory or demeaning comments about a person or group.
- Sexual attention, sexualized material, or unwanted personal contact.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Exposing a private security or conduct report.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or helps review one.
- Spam and other activity that prevents project work.

## Scope

This policy applies to repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and other spaces managed by Vexcalibur. It also applies when someone represents the project in a public space.

## Report a concern

For GitHub content that violates GitHub's policies, use GitHub's **Report content** control.

For a project conduct concern, submit a [private conduct channel request](https://github.com/vexcalibur-dev/.github/issues/new?template=private_conduct_request.yml). The request itself is public, so do not name anyone or describe the incident there. A maintainer will arrange a private channel.

If the concern involves every project maintainer, or if someone is in immediate danger, use [GitHub Support](https://support.github.com/) or the appropriate local emergency service instead of the project form.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, stop an exchange, issue a warning, or restrict participation temporarily or permanently. They will consider the conduct, its effect, prior behavior, and the need to protect participants and project work.

Maintainers will share reports only with people needed to review or act on them. A maintainer involved in a report should not decide its outcome when another maintainer can take over.
